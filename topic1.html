Web Design in the Late 1990s

In the late 1990s, web design was a struggle against limited bandwidth and primitive browsers. Before the widespread adoption of CSS for layout, developers had to repurpose the HTML <table> tag. By nesting tables within tables, designers could force content into columns, creating a grid-like appearance that wouldn't break on different monitors.

Technical Constraints: Dial-up and 8-bit Color

A major driver of 90s aesthetics was the hardware of the time. Most users accessed the internet via dial-up modems with speeds averaging 56kbps. A single high-resolution photograph today is larger than most entire websites were in 1999. To keep load times reasonable, designers relied on "tiled" background patterns, small GIF icons, and a heavy reliance on text over imagery.

Furthermore, developers had to stick to a "Web Safe" palette of 216 colors to ensure consistency across different computer systems. This constraint is why many early sites look remarkably similar in their color choices—primary colors and high-contrast blue links were the only safe bet.
